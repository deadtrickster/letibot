## 1. `268f63ca` - a stopped turn is still woken by any settlement that arrives through the settlement door - PROVED (failing test)

`crates/harnessd/src/jobwatch.rs:924-943` (queue + unconditional ring) vs `:574-586` (the new gate); `crates/harnessd/src/harness/turns.rs:315-322`, `:376-378`; `crates/harnessd/src/harness.rs:1377-1420`; `crates/sessionlog/src/registry.rs:80-89`; `crates/harnessd/src/daemon.rs:360`.

The bug. The commit makes `JobWatchers::turn_ended` ring only when `how == TurnEnd::Done`, but a job or child that settles during the stopped turn has already rung the bell through `SettlementQueue::push` (`jobwatch.rs:940-943`: `g.push_back(c); … self.ring(&self.owner)`); nothing can withdraw a rung wake (`Bell` has `ring_wake` and no un-ring); and the worker's `Work::Woken` arm (`daemon.rs:360` → `Sessions::wake` → `Harness::wake`) then finds the batch in `held` via `between_turns_unread` and runs a turn (`turns.rs:315-322`; `Ok(None)` only when `notices.is_empty()`).

Failure scenario. A turn has a `bash --background` job or a `task` child out. The job ends mid-turn → `push` queues the completion and rings `s-stopped`. The operator presses `esc esc`; the interrupt is taken by the steering, `stop_children_first` settles the children (each ringing too), the turn ends `Interrupted`, and `turn_ended` now correctly stays silent. The court then pops the earlier ring: `wake()` says "job j41 exited 0 …" and a fresh turn runs - the "turn nobody asked for, straight after the one they stopped" that R91.4 set out to remove. The `Failed` arm has the same hole.

Why the commit's test cannot see it. `tests/interrupt_does_not_wake.rs:246` injects the result with `watch.hold(...)` (`jobwatch.rs:624-633`), a door that deliberately does not ring, so only the `turn_ended` ring is under test.

Proof. A copy of that fixture with the settlement delivered through the two acts `push` performs (`watch.queue_for_test(c)` + `registry.bell().ring_wake(owner)`) while the turn streamed, then the operator's stop: `panicked at … R91.4: a settlement that landed in a stopped turn rang a turn of its own`. Fix hint: the between-turns interrupt arm (`sessions.rs:3782-3805`) also leaves pending wakes standing, so the fix probably belongs in the bell (drop this session's wakes), not in `turn_ended`.

## 2. `eacc2f2f` - one close can now block 4 × `GIT_BOUND` = 20 min instead of one 5-minute bound - plausible (arithmetic on the constants)

`crates/tools/src/remote.rs:1990-2004`. `fetch_back` waits up to `GIT_BOUND` (300 s, `:861`) for a slot and then runs for up to `GIT_BOUND`, twice. `rpc::enter`'s contract is the opposite - "never past the call's own deadline" (`rpc.rs:237-239`) - and it only holds if the caller passes its remaining budget, which this one does not.

Scenario. `--stop` closes sessions through `Sessions::close_backends` (`sessions.rs:2323-2343`) → `close_backend` (`harness/models.rs:926-934`) → `SshBackend::settle` (`remote.rs:3067`) → `bring_back` → `fetch_back`, sequentially, with no outer deadline on that chain. On a host whose fetches are slow or dying (8 slots held, each attempt burning 300 s), the ninth close waits 300 s, fails, and reports "`git fetch` got no answer within 300 s … the connection is the suspect" (`:3295-3301`) about a fetch that never started. Twelve sessions then close one after another. `docs/remote-hosts.md:215`, edited by this commit, still advertises "`GIT_BOUND` 5 min for a streamed transfer".

## 4. `e2e6ed83` - the "a question's draft" clear is unconditional on "composer non-empty", and the composer is not always the card's answer - PROVED (two failing tests, control passes)

`crates/tui/src/app/events/asks.rs:13-32` (`clear_composer()` at `:26`); `crates/tui/src/app/events/mod.rs:283-287`; `crates/tui/src/app/composer.rs:112`.

The bug. The function assumes "composer non-empty while a question card is front ⇒ those words are that card's answer". Two ways that is false: (a) `App::submit` gives `!term`, `!send`, `!` and `/` priority over the open decision (`composer.rs:39-109`; the card is only consulted at `:112`), so a command the operator is composing is destroyed; (b) `load` is also the switch path, and `was_asking` is taken from `self.open` while it still holds the outgoing session's cards (`events/mod.rs:283-284`), so leaving a session that is asking you a question clears the line you are typing - and the `!same_session` block (`:96-174`) touches no composer field, so the line would otherwise have been carried across.

Scenario and proof. Three tests over the crate's own fixtures, with a control: control - switch with no question up: line kept (ok); switch away from a front question: `after switch: input="" open=0 notice=Some("switched to two")` → the line is gone and the operator is told nothing; `/queue` typed under a front question, question dropped by a resync: `after resync: input="" notice=Some("resync: test")` → the verb is gone, silently. `ctrl-z` recovers it in both cases, which is why this ranks below the harnessd finding.
