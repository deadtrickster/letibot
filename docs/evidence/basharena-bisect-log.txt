    R12 — an oracle that ran out of room is not an oracle that could not be read
    
    The operator: *"an oracle that ran out of budget is not an oracle that could not be read"*, and
    they asked for the measurement first. **Measured against the store, 2026-09-22** (`adjudication`,
    `arguments_json`/`model_verdict`/`oracle_reply`):
    
        175 rows recorded "gave no verdict this seam could read"
          134  were the model ANSWERING `UNSURE`  — R11's defect (452d7dd re-worded it)
           41  have no verdict line at all
           38  of those end MID-CLAUSE — a word, no full stop: the ceiling's signature
               (21 in this session's daemon, 15 in the other head's, 2 elsewhere)
           the newest two are 11:05 today and 11:23 today, so it is live, not history
          848 oracle consultations since R11, of which 3 recorded the sentence
    
    So the operator's "eleven of sixty-nine" was the count when they wrote it; the store now
    says 41 of 175, and 38 of those are truncations. That is the distribution, and it is why
    the third outcome is worth having.
    
    **Three things, and the first is the one that makes a truncated reply harmless.**
    
    **1. The verdict goes first.** The prompt asked for a sentence and then the verdict on the
    LAST line, so a reply cut before that line was a *budget* recorded as an unreadable answer.
    Now the verdict is asked for on its FIRST line, with the reasoning after it, and the parser
    reads **either end** — so a cut reply still carries its answer and every reply written under
    the old shape still parses. Measured live against this box's guard model
    (`qwen-3.8-27b` at 127.0.0.1:8080), same brief, four calls:
    
        new prompt, max_tokens 120   3 of 3 samples: `ALLOW 0` on line 1, finish_reason stop
        new prompt, max_tokens 8     finish_reason length, content "ALLOW 0\nYes, running the"
    
    The last line is the whole point: eight tokens used to be pure reasoning and no verdict, and
    now the verdict is what survives the cut. (`parse_scores` reads either end for the same
    reason; `verdict_line` asks the *verb* rather than the parse, because `UNSURE` alone parses
    to the same `Verdict::Unsure` an unparseable line does.)
    
    **2. The third outcome, named.** `UnsureKind` — `could_not_decide`, `between_thresholds`,
    `unreadable`, **`out_of_room`** — travels on `OracleAnswer::Unsure { why, kind }`, lands on
    `ModelAdvice` and becomes **a column** (`oracle_reading`, schema **v11**). The sentence and
    the token, because they answer different questions: the sentence is how the operator finds
    out what happened, and the token is how a `GROUP BY` counts *the rate of each* — the same
    move `verdict` makes beside `model_verdict`, and for the reason `store.rs` states
    (*re-parsing prose to recover a label is how a corpus rots*). The ceiling's sentence names
    the budget and the wheel that raises it:
    
        guard ran out of room: the reply stopped at its 120 output-token ceiling before it
        reached a verdict, so this is a BUDGET and not an answer nobody could read.
        `--oracle-max-tokens` raises it, and the bytes are kept either way — `oracle_reply`
        on the corpus row holds what it did write.
    
    **3. The knob that sentence names is real.** `--oracle-max-tokens` (and `oracle.max_tokens`

    which is the defect this whole session has been chasing — a fact named after one thing and
    read from another — so the knob was added rather than the sentence quietly reworded.

    never overrides a verdict: a reply whose first line is `ALLOW 0` is an authorisation whatever
