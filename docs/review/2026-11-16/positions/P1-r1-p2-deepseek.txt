Thesis: accept-and-say is the correct policy, and this tree already applies the right rule in the two places that differ: the store **refuses** by number, because a row an old reader misreads is a silent lie (`tokencore/src/store/floor.rs:39` `READER_ARMS`), while the wire **accepts and says**, because a refusal there strands a live session and the two builds are not the daemon's to judge (`sessionlog/src/server.rs:729-746`). What is wrong is not the policy but its coverage: "translate" is implemented for one carrier and "say" for one binary.

1. **critical — `compat` translates envelopes, never snapshots.** `sessionlog/src/server.rs:2194` builds `OlderHead`; `:2219` writes `a.snapshot` raw into `Hello`; `:2301-2308` writes `Delivery::Resync`'s snapshot raw. `compat.rs:50-84` rewrites `SessionEvent` only. Since 75 `TurnState::Parked` (`view.rs:185`, set at `:214`) exists **only** in a snapshot and describes a turn parked on a usage limit — hours. A head or bridge built before 75 that attaches during a park fails to decode the whole `Hello` (`client.rs:79-90` → `ClientError::Protocol`) and never seats: not read-only, not "everything this head reads is fine" (`protocol.rs:257`). The bridge ships as a per-machine plugin, so those builds are weeks apart. Fix: `OlderHead` owns the snapshot too — for `head_protocol < 75`, rewrite `Parked`→`Interrupted{partial_kept:true}` — applied at `:2219` and `:2301` by one function, and correct the skew sentence.

2. **high — the "say" is a log line, and the head delegated the judgement is the one that never judges.** `server.rs:737` is `eprintln!`, i.e. `$HOME/logs/harnessd-<key>.log` (`scripts/letibot:396`): no warning, no status row, no stored fact. Thirty lines below, the same function says two lesser facts correctly — `hub.warn("prefix_stale")` `:2199`, `hub.warn("store_newer")` `:2204`. Meanwhile rano, the only head whose decoder is hand-written, speaks 72 (`rano src/letibot.rs:26` vs `protocol.rs:15`) and parses `Hello.protocol_version` (`:198`, `:858`) into a field nothing compares. Scenario: every editor pane on a 78 daemon loses 73's held counters and 75's park, and no surface says so. Fix: `hub.warn("protocol_skew", …)` deduped per head at attach; one comparison and one status line in rano; the daemon clamps what it sends an old attached head instead of trusting it.

3. **medium — the lock is one binary's, not the protocol's.** `skew_locked`/`attach_anyway` live in `tui/src/app/session.rs:585,605`. The bridge records skew and keeps writing (`bridge/src/head.rs:823`); acp prints and keeps writing (`acp/src/session.rs:288`); `fleet::prompt_through` (`fleet.rs:217`) never reads the version. Scenario: `Prompt.delivery` (`protocol.rs:609`) was added at 76 with no bump, so a queued prompt reaching a 76 daemon is parsed as the default and **steers** — the operator's held text lands in a running turn. Only the TUI's lock prevents it today. Fix: move the lock beside `protocol_skew` (`protocol.rs:245`) as `Skew{older, locked}` so every `Hello`-seated client inherits it.

4. **medium — the launcher's only skew arm is dead.** `scripts/letibot:1625` matches `*"protocol version"*`; nothing emits that phrase — `protocol.rs:251,259` and `server.rs:379,2149` say "speaks protocol N". Because a modern daemon accepts, `up()` (`:1613-1620`) succeeds and the arm never fires; a refusing daemon prints the decoder sentence, which also does not match, so the operator reads "attached to …" then a head that exits. Fix: read the protocol from `--where --json` (it already reports it) or match `protocol_skew`'s prefix — one source.

Concessions: the unbounded `read_line` (`wire.rs:62`) is real and version-free; I do not defend it, and the cap belongs in `FrameReader`. I also concede position 3's premise, but not its remedy: golden frames would not have caught any of the four — each is a live-path defect (a snapshot, an unmade warning, a missing lock, a string). One socket test per direction, seeded by `sessionlog/tests/late_head.rs:353`, would.

Against the other two: skew *does* change meaning silently (P1-1), and that is precisely why the number cannot be the gate — 76 carries both `TurnEnded` and the unbumped `delivery`, so refusing on the number would refuse the safe send with the unsafe one. Gate on what a head may **send**, not on the number it **is**.
