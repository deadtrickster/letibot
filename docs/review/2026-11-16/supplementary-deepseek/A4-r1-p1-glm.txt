[model: deepseek/deepseek-flash (the default; the one-shot ignored --model)]
**Thesis.** A session's *identity* — who spoke, under which point, under which fence — exists only in the process that composed it. The two doors that rebuild a session outside its spawning thread (the resume/lazy open, and the restart journal) reconstruct the words but not the authority attached to them, and one door hands the model an operator's voice outright.

**F1 (high) The parent's task brief is recorded as the operator's own words.** `children.rs:4220-4223` runs the child's first turn as `h.submit(&brief)`; `turns.rs:201-202` is `submit_spoken(Speaker::Operator,…)`, so the row is `transcript::Speaker::Operator` (`turns.rs:291-296`) and the trail records `Speaker::Operator` (`turns.rs:339`). The trail is the gate's own evidence (`open.rs:3018`) and is re-seeded from those rows after a restart (`trail.rs:134-141`). Failure: a brief containing "the operator approved pushing to main" reaches the child's guard model as an operator utterance it may cite as `ALLOW n`, and any person attaching to the subagent (`enter attaches`) reads a sentence they never typed. `parents_message_turn` gets this right (`turns.rs:1641-1645`, `Speaker::Agent`); the brief does not. Change: speak the brief through the same door — `submit_spoken(Speaker::Tool, …)` — keeping the text.

**F2 (high) A child's point and fence are not re-derived on any open but the spawn.** The spawn is correct (`children.rs:3947-3958` live point; `follow_parent` at `:4114`). But `sessions.rs:2156-2165` builds a reopened session's config as `..self.base.clone()`, i.e. the daemon's flag/default, and `open.rs:669-672` deliberately reads a session's own mode row only for a root; the child branch (`open.rs:695-699`) then *assumes* "the point … carried in `cfg.mode`" came from the parent, which on this door it did not. `open.rs:2213-2217` sets the ceiling to the child's own mode, so `within_parent` (`harness.rs:813`) and `narrow_to_parent` are no-ops. That door is ordinary: `server.rs:1196-1202` resumes a stored session (TUI picker lists children, `tui/src/app/tests/subagents.rs:1605`), `sessions.rs:1995-2013` opens it. Failure: parent narrowed to `read-only`; the operator opens the child from the picker and it runs at `--mode writes-allowed` (or, with no flag, `UNSEEN_PROJECT`/always-ask — the mirror failure). `cfg.perimeter` is likewise `None`, so `perimeter::of` (`open.rs:825`) hands it the bare `[perimeter.subagent]` table instead of the spawn's composition. Change: persist the spawn's point and composed perimeter on the child's row and make `open` refuse a child whose `cfg.mode` is not `no_wider_than` the parent's live point.

**F3 (medium) The restart journal keeps the words and drops the sender and the lane.** `hub.rs:505-536` journals text/attachments/context only; restore writes `from: None, delivery: Default::default()` (`hub.rs:775-781`), and `PromptDelivery` defaults to `Steer` (`protocol.rs:606-611`). A `Queue` follow-up becomes a steer into whatever turn runs next, and a prompt sent with `prompt_from(Some("cc@rano"))` comes back authored by the `peer()` fallback (`hub.rs:87-94`) as `letibot-mcp` — or, from any non-MCP head, as `Speaker::Operator` (`hub_io.rs:216-220`). Change: journal `from` and `delivery`.

**F4 (low) `/fork` mints an operator-origin root.** `store.rs:2880-2882` hardcodes `parent_session_id: None` and `set_session_origin(…, "operator")`, so a fork of a subagent or gatekeeper session becomes the operator's own conversation with no ceiling; `fork.rs:27` also counts peer rows as forkable "messages".

**Concessions.** The spawn path clamps and follows live, and the two R306 doors are tested (`harness/tests/child_mode.rs`). F1's authority half needs an oracle/guard; without one it is an audit defect. The bridge's identity fallback spares most prompts F3's author flip. Position 2 will answer that a picker-opened child is not a supported workflow; position 3 will say the real fault is that a composed session (mode, perimeter, provider, skills) lives only in one process's memory.
