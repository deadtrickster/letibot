### Position 3 - Kimi

```text
[model: kimi-code-global (the preset's default model)]
Thesis: modes are a promise vocabulary, not an authority layer. Mode, permission.json, approved shapes, kept grants, view grants and perimeters compose mostly by early ALLOW, while `no_wider_than` compares only the `Mode` struct. So I6 is structurally unenforceable even after R306: the child's mode narrows, but its effective permissions may not.

1. CRITICAL — `/mode read-only` does not remove capability. `READ_ONLY` claims tools are "absent" (`crates/tools/src/mode.rs:265-277`), but `set_mode_consented` only writes the gate field (`crates/harnessd/src/harness/open.rs` caller path; `crates/harnessd/src/harness.rs:765-800`): no reseat, no backend change, no perimeter change. `Mode.role` is read nowhere in `crates`; seating uses `cfg.seat` (`crates/harnessd/src/harness/open.rs:4745-4746`). The gate never refuses `Disposition::Absent`; it falls through to asking. Worse, permission.json is evaluated before the mode and an `Allow` returns Admit (`crates/tools/src/adjudicate.rs:3325-3381`). Scenario: a coder session is narrowed to read-only, keeps `bash`/`edit`, and an existing exact permission rule runs them with no card. Change: make the Mode the first-class effective ceiling — hard-refuse access where disposition is `Absent`, or reseat on every transition — and never let a subordinate allow precede it.

2. HIGH — narrowing clears grants but not approved shapes, and shapes are project-wide. `AdjudicatedGate::set_mode` clears `grants`, memory and kept `allow`/`memory` rows, but not `approved_shapes` (`crates/tools/src/adjudicate.rs:3098-3112`). A shape returns Admit at `:3493-3505`. Shapes are seeded from every session in the workspace (`crates/harnessd/src/harness/open.rs:3081-3092`; `crates/tokencore/src/store.rs:4195-4230`). Scenario: operator approves `cargo test` in one session; a later child of a read-only/no-exec parent is opened at coder seat, seeds that shape, and runs it unasked. Change: stamp every grant with the authority generation and owning tree; clear shapes on narrowing, and apply the mode before any shape.

3. HIGH — `GrantScope::Once` is not a ceiling. For exec, options are always `exec_options` (`crates/tools/src/adjudicate.rs:2415-2416`); `once_only_options` itself still offers `allow_session` and `allow_always` (`:596-608`), and recording `AllowSession` never reads `mode.grants` (`:3638-3660`). Scenario: a child at always-ask answers one bash card "for the session"; every later matching command is admitted at `:3508-3533`. This directly contradicts I9's comment at `:2406-2408`. Change: derive the ladder and the recording rule from one policy function; under `Once`, show and honour only the once rung.

4. HIGH — the decider axis is outside the ceiling. `no_wider_than` deliberately ignores `decider` (`crates/tools/src/mode.rs:561-586`). A child at `writes allowed` can therefore request `automode-edits` through the bridge; `mode_needs_consent` is false and it applies without a person (`crates/harnessd/src/harness.rs:923-937`), after which the model's admit is the decision (`crates/tools/src/adjudicate.rs:3555-3578`). Scenario: the parent wanted human answers and gets an oracle. Change: order deciders (`None`/Human/Model) inside the ceiling, or require explicit consent to move to `Model`.

Concessions: R306 genuinely blocks numeric mode widening at all three doors, and the live-cell narrowing works; position 1's restart gap is real. Perimeters as an independent filesystem layer are defensible; sandboxing is not authorisation. Early DENY floors are correct. But `allow_once` at always-ask is not the I9 defect — the session rung is.

Anticipating the panel: position 1 lists stale and in-flight widening but misses these early-allow bypasses; position 2's tests remove the mode check and pass, yet never test an effective call under permission.json or a seeded shape, so they prove the comparison, not the ceiling.

```
