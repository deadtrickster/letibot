## Round 1

### A1-r1-p1-opus

```text
A1 R1, reviewer 1, position 1: the loop loses or reorders work. Everything below was found by reading the code at 9e9312b06. No test was run.

THESIS
P4 already covers show-before-store (its A1-A3). This report is about the limit ladder. A park is promised as "Nothing is lost - the daemon opens the turn by itself" (limit.rs:262). That promise is held only in memory. On the default config, a restart during a park drops the owed turn, the operator prompts that wait on it, and any steering the failed round had taken. Nothing tells the operator.

FINDINGS

1. HIGH. A restart during a probed park drops the owed turn.
- `limit_park` is not persisted: it is set to `None` at open (open.rs:3738).
- The probes are on by default at 2, 5 and 15 min (leticode-config/src/lib.rs:537).
- Each probe runs `resume_after_limit` (limit.rs:355). That stores a `User{Speaker::Agent}` notice before the round (turns.rs:1711-1713). The round is refused and the turn parks again.
- `restart::mid_turn` returns false when the last row is a `User{Agent}` row (restart.rs:496-499). So `Owed::any` is false and the restart is quiet.
- Scenario: GLM weekly limit, park, the 2-minute probe is refused. The operator types a prompt, which becomes a row; the next probe puts an Agent notice after it. Daemon restart: no park, no wake. The prompt and the turn are never answered.
- Change: store the park (model, until, reset) on the session row and restore it at open. In `mid_turn`, skip harness rows the same way display-only rows are skipped, and judge by the last non-Agent row.

2. HIGH. Steering taken by a failed round is held only in memory.
- `try_steering_command_of` removes the prompt from the hub queue, and `remove` syncs the journal without it (hub.rs:2263, 487-490).
- After a failed attempt, `give_back` puts it into `handed_back` (hub_io.rs:425). That is a plain `VecDeque` built as `Default` at open (open.rs:3675).
- `run_rounds` says "they go in at the start of the next turn" (turns.rs:1858-1866). The next turn after a park can be hours away (`LONGEST` is 6 h, http/src/reset.rs:11), and the 1 h offline budget can hold it too.
- A restart in that window loses the steer. It exists only in the trail, never in the transcript or the journal.
- Change: put given-back prompts back at the front of the hub queue, so the journal keeps them, instead of in a private buffer.

3. MEDIUM. A restart reorders the queue lane (R290).
- `JournaledPrompt` has no `delivery` field. Restore sets `delivery: Default::default()`, which is `Steer` (hub.rs:776-781, protocol.rs:605-611).
- Scenario: three Tab-queued prompts, then a restart. All three now count as steering (`try_steering_command`, hub.rs:2241-2252). They are drained into whichever turn runs first, for example the restart's wake turn, instead of each getting its own turn in order. HYP on wake-versus-queue ordering.
- Change: journal `delivery`.

4. MEDIUM. A `new_session` fork leaves work behind (R233).
- `limit_new_session` forks `session.items.len()` rows (limit_hook.rs:337-340). It queues the continuation on a hub that `registry.create` has only just made (limit_hook.rs:351-368). The journal is attached only when a harness opens (hub.rs:755): HYP that this new hub has none before the fork opens.
- The old session's `handed_back` and its queued prompts stay on the limited model. Each of them can trigger the hook again, and each trigger is a new fork.
- Change: move the hub queue and `handed_back` into the fork. Make the hook idempotent per episode.

5. LOW. The park "jitter" is a constant.
- `PARK_JITTER_MS` is 2000 (limit.rs:11), and both `park_until` and the probes add it unchanged (limit.rs:241-245). Sessions on one key wake together at reset+2 s and probe together. The ladder's 20% spread from dbe4c3b9c is not used here.
- Change: reuse `Ladder::spread`.

6. LOW. The `Again` arm rebuilds the ladder without `offline_for` (turns.rs:3089-3091). After an `ask_user` answer, a network outage uses up the 5 ordinary retries instead of the 1 h offline budget, and the turn fails.
- Change: use the same constructor as turns.rs:2949.

CONCESSIONS
- The default `on_limit` of Wait (config.rs:1733) is sound inside one daemon life. A first-round park leaves the operator prompt last, so `mid_turn` is true and the wake re-asks it. Finding 1 needs at least one probe.
- Within a live daemon, `handed_back` keeps steering in order (hub_io.rs:186-194). A rule mistake would be found by reading; these failures only show across a restart.
- Show-before-store adds nothing at this level beyond P4's A1/A2.

Expected from position 2: "long GLM sessions survive restarts". The restore path has no park and no `handed_back` (open.rs:3675, 3738), and nothing in the tests covers a restart during a probe. Expected from position 3: "too large to reason about". I agree, and finding 1 shows it: the probe in limit.rs and the restart rule in restart.rs each look correct alone. What breaks is a property neither file owns.
```

### A1-r1-p2-deepseek

```text
[model: deepseek/deepseek-flash (the default; the one-shot ignored --model)]
THESIS. On the three providers that actually bind this box (GLM/z.ai, Kimi coding plan, deepseek), the limit path is one classification and one decision, and it keeps the turn rather than dropping it: a provider refusal keeps its status, body and `Retry-After` (`crates/turn/src/engine.rs:1296-1320`), one `Policy::class` decides (`crates/http/src/retry.rs:155`), one ladder walks it (`crates/harnessd/src/harness/turns.rs:2949`), and exhaustion lands in `after_the_ladder` (`harness/limit.rs:466`) whose default is `Wait` (`config.rs:1733`). The park is a deadline, not a sleep: it is in `next_idle_at` (`sessions.rs:2747`, `:2755`) so the session's own thread wakes at the reset (`sessions.rs:580`), keeps the daemon alive (`sessions.rs:885`), and resumes the same turn (`take_a_due_park`, `harness/limit.rs:272`). The provider prose is tested, not guessed: GLM's wall clock and window (`http/src/reset.rs:248`), Kimi's hyphenated window (`:311`), GLM's business code ahead of money words (`retry.rs:159`), deepseek's "insufficient balance" as a refusal not a wait (`retry.rs:296-316`). End to end: `harnessd/tests/limits.rs:276, 644, 823, 1040, 1062, 1328, 1365` and the hook at `:1555, 1711`.

FINDINGS (each a real residual of this position).

1. `Retry-After` is truncated to 60 s. `http/src/retry.rs:19` `LONGEST_WAIT`, applied at `:196`, against the doc three lines above that says the provider's instant wins when longer. Severity low. Scenario: GLM 429 with `retry-after: 300`; the ladder retries at 60 s, 120 s, each refusal spending a rung, until the ladder is spent and the park it should have started with is reached late, with the refused calls counted against quota. Change: cap by `reset::LONGEST` (6 h) when a header or `from_prose` named an instant, keeping 60 s only for the guessed rungs.

2. The park is process state. `harness/open.rs:3738` sets `limit_park: None`; nothing in `restart.rs` restores it. Severity low-medium. Scenario: reaper or `--stop` 20 min into a 5 h park; the park and `Probing` (`harness/limit.rs:44`) are gone, `restart::mid_turn` (`restart.rs:491`) sees the stored prompt and opens a turn, which eats one refused request and re-parks — the probe ladder restarts from step 0. Change: persist `(model, until_ms, reset_ms)` on the session row exactly as `set_auto_compact_stood_down` does, restore beside `restore_auto_compact` (`sessions.rs:783`).

3. `pre_emptive_compact` disagrees with `measure_resident`. `config.rs:1584` returns false while `ledger_scale` is None, but `harness/wall.rs:294-306` already converts through `carried_scale` — a pair measured on the model before a switch. Severity medium. Scenario: `/models` from GLM to deepseek in a 180k-token session (128k window): the pre-emptive door stands down for the whole first turn, and the session pays a full refused request before `compact_because_refused` (`wall.rs:412`, `turn_tail.rs:281`) folds. Change: admit `carried_scale` in `pre_emptive_compact`.

4. `limit_hook`'s episode file is per model, not per turn. `harness/limit_hook.rs:14, 37` under `daemon_logs()` (`config.rs:2026`), which falls back to `workspace/.letibot` when `logs_root` is None; two sessions on one workspace then share one map, so an `answered` an operator gave session A can decide session B's episode (HYP for a real daemon, which sets `LETIBOT_DAEMON_LOGS`). Severity low. Change: key the file by session, or refuse the fallback.

CONCESSIONS. `restore_after_restart` + `wall.rs:2052` restore the compaction and the ratio (`open.rs:3562-3585`), and `wall_goes_on` bounds continuations (`turn_tail.rs:186`), so I do not claim an unbounded fold loop. But the refusal door never stands down: a session whose own prefix exceeds the window fails every turn until `/compact` or a wider `--context-window`. And a park longer than `LONGEST` is a re-ask, not a wait.

OTHER POSITIONS. Position 1 should attack: show-before-store (`turn/src/engine.rs:487` vs `turns.rs:1713`) and `limit_decide`'s fork — I answer that the fork is `fork_into_new_session` (an explicit conversation fork, `limit_hook.rs:332`), not a cold brief, and that a park resumes the same turn, not a new one. Position 3 is right that the limit story touches five crates; my answer is that only one of them decides, and the split is `provider` → `HttpError` → `http` classifies → `harnessd` decides — one ladder, one park.

```

```text
THESIS
Rate-limit handling is one concept spread over five places: classification in `http/src/retry.rs`, prose parsing in `http/src/reset.rs`, the ladder in `harnessd/src/harness/limit.rs`, the plugin hook in `limit_hook.rs`, and the usage poll in `provider`. - The session stays on the fallback until the operator types `/models`. `limits.rs:1139` covers only a restart mid-park, which re-asks the provider.
- Change: write `limit_return {model, at_ms}` to the session row next to the provider choice, and restore it with `restore_provider_choice`.

2. MEDIUM. `flowy` sends its bearer token in cleartext.
- `http` has no TLS dependency (`http/Cargo.toml`). `flowy/src/creds.rs:387-395` refuses `https://` by name.
- The docs example is `FLOWY_ADDR=http://192.168.1.55:8787` (`creds.rs:455`), so a LAN-wide bearer travels in the clear.
- The model path (`provider`) uses ureq with rustls (`provider/Cargo.toml:23`), so there are two transports with two framings: a hand-rolled chunked/SSE parser in `http/src/lib.rs:416-444` and ureq.
- Change: a loopback-only check in `parse_addr`, or a rustls option. At minimum, refuse a non-loopback host unless the operator explicitly opts in.

3. MEDIUM, HYP on real provider bodies. `Policy::states_a_refusal` (`http/src/retry.rs:296-318`) substring-matches the whole error body for "billing", "credit balance" and similar.
- Any `Status` body containing one of those words becomes `Trouble::Quota` (`retry.rs:216`), whatever the status code.
- A 400 that quotes the user's content (code or docs mentioning billing), or a 429 TPM message that links a billing page, is classified as a spent account.
- `limit_answer` then returns `GiveUp` (`limit.rs:213`), so the turn fails instead of retrying or parking.
- Change: match only on the provider's structured error code field, or restrict the substring match to 402 and 429 bodies, with a test per provider.

4. LOW. `Trouble::RateLimit` includes 408 (`retry.rs:220`), a request timeout. That is a client-side or network fault, and it can park a session on a prose-derived reset. Change: map 408 to `Timeout`.

5. LOW. Park state (`limit_park`, `limit_probing`, `limited`) is all in memory (`harness.rs:510-515`).
- After a restart, `mid_turn` re-asks the provider, which costs one refused request per session. Many parked sessions mean a thundering herd at the restart.
- Change: persist `reset_ms` with the provider choice and skip the re-ask until then.

6. LOW. A parked session blocks daemon idle-exit for up to 6 h (`sessions.rs:888`, `LONGEST` at `http/src/reset.rs:11`). That may be intended. Document it, or let idle-exit proceed since the park is resumable.

7. Structure (position 3's core).
- `harness/turns.rs` is 5997 lines and `turn/src/engine.rs` is 1986. The limit logic is split over `limit.rs`, `limit_hook.rs`, `turns.rs:3571-3640` (the fallback), `usage.rs`, `wall.rs` and `provider/src/usage_poll.rs`.
- `after_the_ladder` calls the hook first (`limit.rs:472`), then `limit_answer`, then the fallback. The hook's `ep.fell_back` flag (`limit_hook.rs:117`) is a second state machine beside `limit_park`, `limit_return` and `limited`.
- Change: one `LimitState` struct, persisted as a unit (this fixes 1 and 5), with `limit_hook`'s episodes as a field. Move the fallback into `limit.rs`.

CONCESSIONS
- To position 2: the ladder is well tested for park, switch and return within one daemon life (`limits.rs`, 20+ scenarios). Per-session hook state is correctly keyed (`daemon_logs` includes the session id; I checked a possible cross-session clobber and it does not exist). A restart mid-park resumes the owed turn (`limits.rs:1138`). The Kimi/GLM parsing, including GLM China time, is deliberate and tested.
- To position 1: show-before-store and the lost-prompt window are real, but P4 already covers them (A1/A2) and I add nothing. The `on_limit` default of Wait is conservative and safe.
- On TLS: the `http` crate is deliberately local and LAN plaintext (`embeddings` uses curl for https). It is a boundary issue only for `flowy`.

ANTICIPATED REBUTTAL
"Large files are not bugs." The size is what let findings 1 and 5 hide: the persisted half (provider choice) and the in-memory half (return, limited) of one decision sit in different files and are never read together. The `LimitState` unification above is the concrete fix, not a style preference.
```
