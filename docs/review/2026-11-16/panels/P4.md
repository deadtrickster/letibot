## Round 1

### P4-r1-p1-glm.txt

```text
[model: glm-coding (the preset's default model)]
P4 · Position 1 · A crash loses rows or prompts

THESIS
The durability machinery (WAL+FULL, append-only triggers, journal) protects only what reaches the store. Four verified paths publish or accept conversation ahead of the write that makes it durable, and the restore paths rebuild from the store, so the loss is silent: after a restart the operator's screen and the record disagree and nothing says so.

FINDINGS

F1 Show-before-store, minutes wide at the round seam — high.
`turn/src/engine.rs:487` emits `TranscriptAppended` inside `append_items`; `CapturingSink` forwards live ("Forwards everything", `harnessd/src/harness/turns.rs:92-95`). At a round boundary the rows are appended and reconciled at `turns.rs:2582-2584`, then `serve_reviews` (`:2604`, runs reviewer gates), `apply_queued_head_run` (`:2600`, runs operator tools), jobs and mode application run, and only then `persist()` at `:2611`. Scenario: daemon SIGKILLed (reaper grace is 5 s, `tools/src/lifecycle/reap.rs:52`) during a review in that gap — the head drew the whole round, the store has none of it, and `republish_after` (`harnessd/src/harness/open.rs:4269`) re-announces from the store, so the row vanishes without a warning. Untested (invariants I1). Change: persist before the side work, and on open warn when a head's last seen item_id is absent from the store.

F2 `Accepted` before the journal write; nobody waits — high.
`push_back`→`sync()` posts to the journal thread ("Never waits on the store", `sessionlog/src/hub.rs:527-537`), then `Accepted` returns at `:1994`. The thread's `save` (`:431`) can sit out the 90 s busy ladder (`tokencore/src/store/busy.rs:12`) while the reaper kills at 5 s. `journal_settled` is called only by tests (`harnessd/src/restart.rs:2066,2078`); the surviving-prompt test settles *before* killing (`hub.rs:2521-2524`), so the real window is untested by construction. Change: settle (bounded) before `Accepted`, or before `pop_front` hands a prompt to a turn.

F3 Room items store: skip-then-compact erases; closed-segment failure drops — medium.
`room/src/store.rs:100-127` skips unparseable lines to stderr; `compact` (`:373-416`) rewrites the file from the shadow, permanently erasing every skipped row. The JSONL has no version (S4), so one record-shape change makes every old line unparseable — a restart drops the room's whole item history. `close` (`:299`) ignores a failed append while the rows are already trimmed from the ring: gone after the next compaction. Change: version records; quarantine bad lines to a sidecar; retry failed closes.

F4 Migration: no transaction, no cross-process lock — medium.
`tokencore/src/store.rs:1848-1922`: arms are separate autocommits; v2/v4 ALTERs (`:1932-1933`, `:1951-1952`) have no existence check, unlike v45+ (`:2539-2559`). A crash between an old ALTER and the stamp re-runs it → "duplicate column name" → every daemon's open fails: record intact, unreachable. Between the stamp's DELETE (`:1870`) and INSERT (`:1871`) a concurrent opener reads an empty version table as a fresh store and skips `migrate_from`/`repair_queue_mute`. Change: wrap arms+stamp in one `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`; SQLite DDL is transactional.

CONCESSIONS
Position 2: FULL sync plus the no-UPDATE/no-DELETE triggers (`store.rs:526-538`, verified) do make stored bytes durable, recent arms are idempotent, SCHEMA_SQL is `IF NOT EXISTS` (so the concurrent fresh-path is table-benign), and per-round persist bounds most loss — F1/F2 need a kill inside the window. Position 3: agreed that only warnings have a destination table; F1's silent divergence is exactly the structural gap you name, and the journal dropping `from` (`hub.rs:773-779`) also mutates the restored record.

```

### P4-r1-p2-fable.txt

```text
P4 R1, reviewer 2 (position 2: the store makes loss practically impossible). No test run; all by reading at 9e9312b06.

THESIS
A committed row cannot be lost. What can be lost is a row not yet committed, and the fixes are small. Position 1 is right about windows, wrong about the store.

What holds (verified):
- Every writing connection takes WAL + synchronous=FULL (tokencore/src/store.rs:1605-1611). Only embeddings/metrics use NORMAL (embeddings/src/cache.rs:95, metrics/src/store.rs:213); neither holds conversation.
- UPDATE/DELETE on transcript_item abort, INSERT must be next seq (store.rs:527-541). Appends are BEGIN IMMEDIATE, one commit per 64-row chunk (store.rs:3120, 4343-4354).
- No migration arm touches transcript rows: the only DELETE in migrate_from is the v23 queue clear (store.rs:2341). A half-run migration costs availability, not rows.
- The single trigger drop on a live file (tokencore/src/migrate_split.rs:313-317) is inside one transaction, after verify_chains (:274), restored at :352.
- Busy store: persist waits 30 min, resyncs `persisted` from the store (harnessd/src/harness/turns.rs:4480, open.rs:391).

FINDINGS

1. HIGH - prompt leaves the journal before the transcript holds it. pop_front syncs at once (sessionlog/src/hub.rs:481-485, 528-537); the row is stored only at harnessd/src/harness/turns.rs:1713. In between: reseat, render, and a persist that can wait 30 min. Scenario: store busy, operator restarts the daemon; prompt is in neither queued_prompts nor transcript_item. Head re-send: HYP. Change: keep the popped prompt journaled as in-flight; remove it in the `opened` callback, dedupe on client_request_id at restore.

2. HIGH - "stored" is marked when nothing was stored. On a queued interrupt during a busy wait persist_rows returns Ok with rows unwritten (turns.rs:4507, 61-66). submit_items_then then calls opened (turns.rs:1713-1714) -> the_batch_stored -> watch.stored + room ack_through (harness.rs:1404-1408, harness/deliveries.rs:1051-1057). Scenario: Esc during busy store, then restart: job result and room lines acked, row never written, never told again. Breaks R313/I53. Change: return a not-stored outcome on Leave; call opened only when persisted == rows.len().

3. MEDIUM - journal is acked before saved, never awaited. sync() only sends (hub.rs:528-537); journal_settled has no non-test caller (hub.rs:809; only harnessd/src/restart.rs:2066,2078 tests). Scenario: prompt accepted, daemon killed within the fsync. Change: wait journal_settled (bounded) before acking and on close.

4. MEDIUM - room board file can be wiped by one bad byte. Store::open uses read_to_string(...).unwrap_or_default() (room/src/store.rs:92): invalid UTF-8 or EIO reads as an empty store; bytes=0, and after 64 KiB of appends compact rewrites the file from that shadow (room/src/store.rs:373-378, 414-416). Same path makes a skipped line (:122-126) permanent. The closed reader already uses from_utf8_lossy (:306-307). Change: lossy read; refuse to compact after a load that failed or skipped; keep the old file as .bad.

5. LOW - room store never fsyncs (append :353-357, compact :416). Process crash safe; power loss loses recent board rows and can leave an empty file after rename. Change: sync_data on append, fsync tmp + dir on compact.

6. LOW - show before store (turn/src/engine.rs:487 vs turns.rs:1713). Real, but only harms through 1 and 2; with those fixed the loser is a drawn row whose prompt comes back from the journal.

CONCESSIONS
- "Practically impossible" is false for accepted-but-uncommitted input: 1-3 are plain process-death windows, no power loss needed. Position 1 wins there.
- Journal drops `from` (hub.rs:458-470, 779): a restored bridge prompt changes speaker. Not loss, but the record is altered.
- Split set: rows and context are two commits (store.rs:3162-3176); crash between leaves stale context counters, not lost rows.
- Position 3: nothing I verified stops an operator-only row; triggers guard row survival, not row meaning. Out of my position's reach, not refuted.
- Carve claim check is check-then-act against a daemon starting mid-carve: HYP, not traced.
```

### P4-r1-p3-kimi.txt

```text
[model: kimi-code-global (the preset's default model)]
Thesis: I2 is enforced by two hand-maintained tables, not by the record’s type. The warning destination table covers only warnings in `TABLE`; `TranscriptItem` still admits operator-only rows, and the “twin” table is prose, not evidence that the model got a row. The implementation is poor for that reason regardless of whether today’s workloads lose data.

Findings:

1. Operator-only rows are a first-class variant. `crates/transcript/src/lib.rs:219-225` defines `Offer` as “display-only”; `crates/harnessd/src/restart.rs:504-509` classes it and `SegmentMark` as rows “the model never reads”; the head draws them at `crates/tui/src/ui/transcript/item.rs:228-237`, while every request renderer discards them (`crates/provider/src/messages.rs:377`). Scenario: the shadow allows a read and appends its `Offer` row (`crates/harnessd/src/harness/shadow_seat.rs:38-51`); the operator sees an authorisation the model was never told about, and a head-only fact is now in the model transcript’s row numbering. Severity high. Change: remove display-only data from `TranscriptItem`; give head-only facts their own durable event/type, or make the row door take an explicit audience and have request renderers reject head-only rows at compile time.

2. The destination table is incomplete and string-typed. `Hub::warn` accepts any `String` (`crates/sessionlog/src/hub.rs:1095-1112`); an unknown code is deliberately Routine/Notes (`warning.rs:1284-1302`). `lifecycle()` emits `format!("lifecycle_{event}")` (`crates/harnessd/src/harness/open.rs:3879,3924`), which `warning_codes.rs:126-167` cannot discover from a format string and `TABLE` does not name. Scenario: a lifecycle layer’s failed `room_say` arrives as routine `/notes` text, occasionally and untested. Severity medium. Change: a warning-code newtype constructed only from a registered table, with exhaustive destination computed at construction; dynamic families must register a prefix rather than bypass the census.

3. Warnings moved “beside” the conversation have no durable copy. `SessionView` keeps at most 128 warnings in memory and drains the oldest (`crates/sessionlog/src/view.rs:339-350,761-768`); restart republishes transcript rows only (`crates/harnessd/src/harness/open.rs:4245-4276`); metrics records the code but not the detail (`crates/sessionlog/src/metrics_tap.rs:108-115`). Scenario: `operator_shell_failed` or `todos_not_stored` is raised, then the daemon restarts; `/notes` loses the sentence that said what failed and how to recover. Severity high. Change: persist every disclosure in an append-only table with fact id, code, detail, time and audience, replay it into snapshots, and age it out by archive rather than deletion.

4. Proving a model twin does not prove the model’s row exists. `TWIN` maps a code to an explanatory string (`warning.rs:1151-1203`); its test only checks that string is long and the code classified (`:1505-1517`). The TUI test synthesises both rows (`crates/tui/src/app/tests/onevent.rs:36-58`), so a production path that stopped writing the notice stays green while `Go::Model` suppresses the warning. Severity medium. Change: make the notice and warning one event carrying both audiences, or bind each twin to a real event constructor and assert the production pair end to end.

Concessions: Position 1 is right that emission precedes persistence (`crates/turn/src/engine.rs:486-493`, `crates/harnessd/src/harness/turns.rs:1711-1713`); reordering alone would not cure findings 3-4, and shadow rows are discarded after `take_rows` when append fails (`shadow_seat.rs:50,374`). Position 2 is right that append-only triggers and `synchronous=FULL` protect committed SQLite rows (`tokencore/src/store.rs:526-534,1602-1609`); they cannot protect rows not yet stored, in-memory warnings, or a head-only display.

```

#### /data/review-ext/as-deepseek/P4-r1-p1-glm.txt

```text
[model: deepseek/deepseek-flash (the default; the one-shot ignored --model)]
THESIS. The hub is the authority and the store is a follower. `append_items` publishes `TranscriptAppended` from inside the ledger loop (`turn/src/engine.rs:487`), and `submit_items_then` stores afterwards (`harnessd/src/harness/turns.rs:1711` then `:1713`). Nothing in the tree can un-announce a row — `SessionEvent` has no retraction — so every gap below is a row the operator saw and the store does not have, permanently. I1 is not "practically impossible"; it holds for committed rows and fails at every seam where the two orders disagree.

FINDINGS

1. Show before store, no withdrawal. `turn/src/engine.rs:487` emits per item; `turns.rs:1713` `self.persist()?`. A non-busy store error (SQLITE_IOERR, a split file gone) returns from `submit_items_then` after the head has drawn the prompt. Severity high. Scenario: `<data>/projects/<key>.db` is deleted under a running daemon mid-turn; the head shows the user row, the turn fails, a restart opens the parent with no such row. Change: persist the chunk first and emit after the commit (the ledger row already carries the id and head), or add a retraction event and use it.

2. The busy store's "written when it frees" is false. `turns.rs:4504-4507` returns `Ok` with rows unwritten; the notice at `:4495-4500` promises they are "written when it frees", but no thread retries — only a later `persist()` from the same harness (`:4439` resumes at `persisted`). A stopped turn that is the session's last, then a `--restart` (`daemon.rs:205` `exit(130)`), loses them. Severity high. Change: one owed-write record that is either re-driven by the daemon's idle pass or said as lost.

3. The prompt journal has no shutdown barrier. `sync` only `send`s (`sessionlog/src/hub.rs:528-537`); `journal_settled` (`:809`) has zero production callers (only `hub.rs:2521,2537` and `restart.rs:2066`, both tests); `Hub::close` (`:2368`) and `Registry::close` (`registry.rs:2563`) never flush; the signal thread exits by `std::process::exit` after `SERVE_EXIT_GRACE` (`daemon.rs:70,205`). A prompt accepted while a failed save is retrying on its one-second timer (`hub.rs:443`) is lost by the very `--restart` it was queued for. Severity medium. Change: `journal_settled(2s)` in `Hub::close`, before `Bye`.

4. Room history is destroyed by the store's own healing. `put_item` trims, then `close()`es the row (`room/src/store.rs:176-186`); `append` failing is only an `eprintln` (`:360-366`); `write` then `compact()`s (`:339`), and `compact` rewrites the live file from the shadow (`:373-424`) which no longer holds the trimmed row. Neither file is ever fsynced (no `sync_all` in `crates/room`). `open` (`:91-139`) skips a torn line and does not repair a missing trailing `\n`, and `append` is `O_APPEND` (`:353-357`), so the first record written after a restart glues onto the fragment and is lost too. Severity high — the module doc claims "a failed write is healed by the next compaction", which is exactly backwards for a trimmed row. Change: close before trim commits nothing until `.items-closed` has the bytes, and repair the trailing newline in `open`.

5. Migration is not one transaction and two arms are not idempotent. `store.rs:1932` and `:1951` are bare `ALTER TABLE ... ADD COLUMN`; the version is stamped once at `:1870-1874`. Every later arm is pragma-guarded, so only these two brick — and `StoreSet::create` (`store/split.rs:442-459`) has no cross-process lock, so two daemons opening one `shared.db` after an upgrade can race. A crash or the loser gets `duplicate column name` on every subsequent open. Severity medium.

CONCESSIONS. The append-only triggers (`store.rs:526-538`), `synchronous=FULL` (`:1608`), the atomic journal replace (`store/prompts.rs:41-67`), the pre-fork flush (`wall.rs:1721`) and `delete_empty_session`'s refusal (`:3980-3992`) are real and good.

ANTICIPATION. Position 2 will call these microsecond windows; findings 2, 3 and 4 are not crashes at all, they are ordinary paths. Position 3 will say skew is unprovable; I hand them the missing tests: no failure-injected `persist` with a drawn row, no restart with a torn trailing line, no two-daemon migration.

```

[model: deepseek/deepseek-flash (the default; the one-shot ignored --model)]
THESIS. Positions 1 and 2 both argue about *loss*. Neither argues about *identity*, which is what I2 actually states ("that row is exactly what the model was sent"). At 9e9312b06 the tree keeps a first-class class of rows that no model is ever sent, inside the append-only table, drawn by every head, and the only thing standing between them and the rule is a per-renderer `match` arm. The warning destination table is keyed on warning codes and covers nothing else.

F1 (high). `TranscriptItem::Offer` is an operator-only row inside the record, and its classification is written three times, unconnected. It is appended through the same door as model rows — `harnessd/src/harness/turns.rs:3865-3877` → `turn/src/engine.rs:458-495`, which mints an `item_id` and a ledger row and emits `TranscriptAppended` — is skipped by every dialect (`dialect-qwen/src/render.rs:276`, `dialect-glm/src/render.rs:226,480`) and by the import path (`claude/src/handover.rs:265-285`), is drawn in place (`tui/src/ui/transcript/item.rs:228`, `bridge/src/rows.rs:94`), and is named by a second, independently written predicate: `harnessd/src/restart.rs:504` "A row the model never reads, filed by the harness for the operator". Failure: the shadow's answered-read card (`harnessd/src/harness/shadow_seat.rs:43`) carries the quoted permission rule and evidence, so operator policy text is stored, numbered and drawn inside the conversation the model was sent; and `claude/src/handover.rs:389` renders the same row as `> *title*` into a foreign conversation the native dialects hide — one row, two answers, no test that the sets agree. Change: put the predicate on the type in `crates/transcript` (`TranscriptItem::reaches_model()`, exhaustive), and make every renderer, `handover`, `restart::display_only` and `harness/open.rs:4339` call it.

F2 (medium). The one consumer that must be right has no coverage. `restart::mid_turn` (`restart.rs:491-509`) and `owed_after_a_restart` (`harness/turns.rs:1454`) read back past display-only rows to decide whether a restart says "you were mid-turn". Only `restart.rs:1313` covers today's two kinds. A new operator-only kind, or an offer appended at a new seam, silently inverts the restart reminder — a wasted turn or a missing one. Change: filter on `reaches_model()` and assert the set once.

F3 (medium). `destination()` (`sessionlog/src/warning.rs:1269`) is total over `TABLE` and total over nothing else: the tests at `warning.rs:1555` and `tui/src/app/tests/onevent.rs:172` walk warning codes only, while `SessionEvent` has ~45 variants and every non-warning event drawn as a row is unchecked (`ProviderTrouble`'s "one row" rule is hand-coded at `tui/src/app/events/warnings.rs:326-346`). Nothing structural stops a second operator-only row; F1 is proof one exists.

F4 (medium). `Go::Model` is a hand-written claim nothing verifies, and for `target_red` it is false on most sessions that receive it. The code is published to every hub of the repository (`mergequeue/serve.rs:2048` → `mergequeue.rs:832-851`, gatekeeper and children included) while its twin, the `EMERGENCY` notice, is delivered only to `roots_of` (`mergequeue.rs:578-593`, which excludes both). On a child or gatekeeper head the promised row is on another session's transcript, and the head draws nothing (`warnings.rs:275-276`). Change: the raise site names the twin's row id; a test walks `TWIN` and asserts the row exists after the event.

F5 (low). `delete_empty_session` (`tokencore/src/store.rs:3980-3986`) refuses any session holding rows, so a session whose only rows are offers can never be removed.

CONCESSIONS. Position 1's loss paths are real for rows, not for prompts. Position 2 is right that loss is hard here: WAL `synchronous=FULL`, the append-only triggers and archive-before-delete hold. Offer rows are intentional (the operator wanted clickable cards), so the fix is classification, not deletion — which is exactly why neither loss argument reaches it.
