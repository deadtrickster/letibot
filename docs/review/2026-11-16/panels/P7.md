```text
P7 reviewer 1 - the launcher misroutes

Thesis. `scripts/letibot` makes routing decisions that the Rust side either does not share or contradicts, and `tests/launcher.sh` cannot see them. The tests run under `env -i HOME=... XDG_RUNTIME_DIR=...` (tests/launcher.sh:105,252), so XDG drift and `LETIBOT_PROVIDERS` are never exercised. None of the verbs `hold`, `release` or `where` appears in a case.

Findings

1. HIGH. `hold`, `release` and `where` become paid one-shot prompts. The verb arm is `stats | boundary | fb | holds | gc` (scripts/letibot:2737) and has no `hold`, `release` or `where`, although the multicall has all three (crates/letibot/src/main.rs:299-307). An unknown first word falls through to `harnessd --prompt "$*"` (scripts/letibot:2993-3005). The reaper's own warning tells the operator to type exactly this: "`letibot hold {}` keeps it" (crates/tools/src/lifecycle/reap.rs:680, sessionlog/src/warning.rs:987). The help lists `where` and `gc` as verbs (scripts/letibot:897-898,931). Scenario: the idle-close warning fires and the operator runs `letibot hold s-123`. A billed model turn answers the sentence "hold s-123", no hold is written, and the session closes five minutes later. `gc_now.rs` calls the binary directly, so nothing catches this. Fix: add `hold | release | where` to the :2737 arm, plus a launcher.sh case that asserts every word in main.rs's verb chain routes to `letibot-multi`. Better still, generate the arm from the verb list.

2. MEDIUM-HIGH. The launcher ignores `XDG_DATA_HOME` for the store. The launcher uses `STORE="${LETIBOT_STORE:-$HOME/.local/share/letibot}"` (scripts/letibot:374). Rust uses `$XDG_DATA_HOME/letibot` first: `tools/src/lifecycle.rs:343 data_dir`, `bridge/src/daemon.rs:90 default_store`, and `letibot/src/gc.rs:1070 open_for_acts`. The room at :1948 does honour XDG. Scenario: with XDG_DATA_HOME set, the daemon writes `~/.local/share/letibot`, while `letibot gc`, `holds`, `hold` and the bridge act on `$XDG_DATA_HOME/letibot`. gc finds no owners and the hold is invisible to the daemon (HYP on the reaper side). A sandboxed run meant to have its own data home writes into the operator's store. Fix: `${XDG_DATA_HOME:-$HOME/.local/share}/letibot`, or ask `letibot-multi where --store`.

3. MEDIUM. Nothing in Rust reads `LETIBOT_PROVIDERS`, and the launcher ignores `XDG_CONFIG_HOME` for providers. The launcher reads `PROVIDERS="${LETIBOT_PROVIDERS:-$HOME/.config/letibot/providers.toml}"` (scripts/letibot:1095), and its comment says this "points both readers". The daemon reads `provider/src/keys.rs:194 config_file` (XDG first, no env door). The launcher derives `--endpoint` from its own file through `model_home` (:1387-1408) and the banner model through `answers_now` (:1509). Scenario: a test or operator setting `LETIBOT_PROVIDERS=/x.toml` gets the endpoint from /x and the keys and [default] from the real file. That is a mixed provider, and the record names the wrong model. Fix: pass `--providers FILE` to harnessd, or drop the variable and have the launcher ask the daemon's resolver.

4. MEDIUM. The real `systemd-run` after the probe has no bound. `scope_run` (:317) builds `systemd-run --user --scope --unit ... --slice ... -p Delegate=yes` with no timeout. The 2 s probe only proves that `systemd-run ... true` answered. Scenario: the manager stalls on the slice or the named unit. The one-shot `exec`s it (:2998) and hangs the terminal without limit. For a daemon start, the `DPID` loop waits 300 s (:3083), exits "did not come up", and leaves the orphaned systemd-run to start a harnessd later, which then races the next launcher for the lock. Fix: wrap it in `timeout` and fall back to the unscoped path on expiry, or move the scope join into `late_scope.rs`.

5. So `--model` and `--provider` from the original start are dropped, which `restart_advice` (:419) itself knows to carry (HYP whether session rows restore them). Fix: an interrupt flag that sets stopping first, and carry the record's model and provider into the restart.

Concessions. `--config-reference`, `doctor`, `browser`, `rooms`, `notes` and `shadow` route correctly and guard prompt-shaped words. The probe is backgrounded and bounded. I42 holds at dist (not re-verified).

Against position 2: launcher.sh and no_hang.rs pass because their environment removes exactly the variables that drift. A green run there says nothing about findings 1-3. Against position 3: rewriting this in Rust is right long-term, but findings 1 and 2 are small fixes needed today. In the meantime, a verb-list parity test is the cheap guard.
```

### P2 - DeepSeek
```text
[model: deepseek/deepseek-flash (the default; the one-shot ignored --model)]

**Findings.**

Failure: an editor panel shelling out `letibot where`, or an operator calling the documented verb, gets neither answer. Fix: one `where | hold | release)` arm beside `:2737`, or a `*)` refuse for any first word the multicall implements; and walk `:931`'s VERBS line from `tests/launcher.sh` the way `:427-438` walks the flag list.

2. The launcher and the daemon read different `providers.toml` — **high**. `scripts/letibot:1095` hardcodes `$HOME/.config/letibot/providers.toml` and ignores `XDG_CONFIG_HOME` (which `:2019` honours elsewhere); `crates/provider/src/keys.rs:194-200` honours it. A checkout holding `letibot/providers.toml` then supplies the daemon's provider and defaults. `crates/plugins/src/paths.rs:36-43` states and implements the opposite rule, and `crates/leticode-config/Cargo.toml:20-23` asserts there is one such function. Fix: call that function.

4. `no_hang.rs` never tests the case it exists for — **low**. The `systemd-run` stub answers at 6 s (`crates/letibot/tests/no_hang.rs:104-107`, "so the backgrounded probe ends inside the test"), while `scripts/letibot:226-249` deliberately leaves a timed-out probe running and `:235-236` reaps only probe dirs that *have* an answer — so a wedged manager leaks one dir per launcher run, forever. Fix: a stub that never answers, and an `-mmin` sweep that also drops unanswered dirs older than the daemon's own wait.

**Concessions.** `check-dist-names.sh` is genuinely two-directional (`:44-115`, `:127-145`, `:174-192`) — but its `triple:` grep drops `$`-interpolated matrix values (`:36`), so a renamed workflow key vanishes silently. **Against the others.** Position 1 names three verbs; the defect is the missing `*)` arm, so the next multicall verb repeats it — and it misses the config split, which makes the launcher's *reports* wrong, not just its dispatch. Position 3 is right that 3,173 lines of placement is too much bash, but a port would not have caught finding 1: the dispatch table would still be a whitelist, and it is the launcher suite that must walk it.
```

### P3 - Claude sonnet
```text
P7, position 3 (reviewer 3). Placement logic in bash should be a Rust verb.

THESIS
scripts/letibot is 3173 lines. Before any Rust runs it decides workspace, key, rundir, store, providers file, endpoint, scope and daemon start. Each of these is a second implementation of something Rust already owns, and the pairs agree only where tests/launcher.sh:601 pins them. P7 positions 1 and 2 (misroutes, hang probes) are symptoms of that duplication. Every verb, path or parser added in Rust needs a bash twin, or it falls through to a billed prompt. The seam list itself says the verbs live outside REGISTRY and nothing checks them against the launcher (seams S14). The launcher should shrink to a small exec shim (about 100 lines: resolve `$0`, exec `letibot-multi launch`). Rust already has `bridge/src/daemon.rs` (key, data_dir, Daemon) and `provider::keys::config_file`.

FINDINGS

1. HIGH. Three resolvers pick the providers file, and they disagree.
- Launcher: `PROVIDERS="${LETIBOT_PROVIDERS:-$HOME/.config/letibot/providers.toml}"` (scripts/letibot:1095). It ignores `XDG_CONFIG_HOME`.
- Rust: `provider/src/keys.rs:194-201 config_file` honours `XDG_CONFIG_HOME` and never reads `LETIBOT_PROVIDERS`. My grep over crates found no Rust read of that variable.
- The launcher comment at :1093 says `LETIBOT_PROVIDERS` "points both". It does not.
- Failure: a box sets `XDG_CONFIG_HOME=/x`. The launcher finds no file and reports PROV_MISSING="file" (:1385). The daemon reads `/x/letibot/providers.toml` and has the model. Reverse case: `LETIBOT_PROVIDERS=/alt/p.toml` makes the launcher resolve the endpoint and `default_provider` from /alt, while the daemon loads `~/.config`. Turns then go to a different model than the one the launcher announced.
- Change: Rust owns one `config_file()`. The launcher asks `letibot-multi --config-path`, or the launcher is deleted.

2. HIGH. The awk TOML reader (`model_home`, scripts/letibot:1124-1175) claims to mirror `local_models` "rule for rule". It does not.
- `keys.rs:850 parse_file` returns Err on any line that is not `key=value` or `[section]`. `local_models` (:1059-1061) then returns an empty Vec, so the Rust side sees no models. The awk skips such lines (`if (eq == 0) next`) and still reports homes.
- Rust merges duplicate `[model."x"]` sections into one map entry (`entry(sec).or_default().insert`). The awk emits one record per block.
- Failure: a file with a duplicate alias block, or one stray line such as a multiline array continuation. Rust sees one merged model, or none. The launcher prints "listed at more than one address" (:1386-1412), picks the on-box one and passes `--endpoint`. The daemon's own fleet view disagrees. Only a rule-by-rule test pins them, and tests/launcher.sh cannot parse the Rust side.
- Change: delete awk. Add `letibot-multi model-home ALIAS`, which uses `local_models`.

3. MEDIUM. Data-dir and store resolution is split.
- `STORE="${LETIBOT_STORE:-$HOME/.local/share/letibot}"` (:374) ignores `XDG_DATA_HOME`.
- The room uses `${XDG_DATA_HOME:-...}` (:1948). `tools/src/lifecycle.rs:344` and `bridge/src/daemon.rs:100` also use `XDG_DATA_HOME`.
- Failure: `XDG_DATA_HOME` set. The launcher's `STORE_FILE` (:386, used by `letibot migrate` at :2745) and its store-split checks (:377-391) look in `~/.local/share`. The daemon and room use XDG. `migrate` moves a session in the wrong store.
- The seam list already admits this ("ignores XDG_DATA_HOME except for the room").

4. MEDIUM. Key parity rests on one test.
- Shell: `printf '%s' "$WORKSPACE" | sha12` where WORKSPACE comes from `$(cd ... && pwd -P)` (:123-126). `$(...)` strips trailing newlines.
- Rust: `bridge/src/daemon.rs:58 key` hashes `as_encoded_bytes`.
- A path ending in a newline, or a symlink resolved differently by Rust canonicalize, gives the head a different socket than the launcher. This is low-frequency but silent (the head waits on a socket that never appears).
- Parity relies on tests/launcher.sh:601 plus a doc comment. Rust can compute the key, so no test is needed once the launcher is Rust.

5. MEDIUM. The hang gaps in I28 exist because the shell is the supervisor. `detach` (:337-347) runs `scope_run` and then `setsid "$@"` with no bound after a good probe. Bash has no reliable bound for `systemd-run --scope` (`timeout` is absent on macOS, and `probe_bounded` falls back to unbounded, :219-226). The probe machinery (about 100 lines, :212-336, with race files under `$RUNDIR/scope-probe-$$` and a `find -mmin +60 -exec rm -rf` sweep at :236) exists to work around the language. Rust already has `activation/bounded.rs`.

6. LOW. The launcher is 3173 lines with 79 functions, and about 750 lines of those are flag and help tables (`flags_table` :752, `removed_flags_table` :816). `flag_field`, `known_flag` and `flag_takes_value` hand-roll a parser. Rust (clap, or the existing `cli.rs`) would generate help and refusals from one source. This is the recurring "a bare word is a paid prompt" class: `hold`, `release` and bare `where` have no arm at :2737, and no check can catch that.

ANTICIPATED COUNTERS
- Position 1 will list misroutes. I say they are inevitable while two verb tables exist (the REGISTRY and the bash `case` at :2737). Fixing them one by one leaves the next verb broken.
- Position 2 will add tests. A test can show agreement on its inputs. It cannot show agreement for `XDG_CONFIG_HOME`, duplicate sections or malformed lines, which are the cases in findings 1 and 2.
- Cost: the launcher must run before the binary exists, and `--help` must survive a broken install. Answer: a 100-line shim does `exec` and nothing else. Rust owns every decision.

CONCESSIONS
- Bash shims are the right choice for exec-only wrappers (`scripts/leticode` at 35 lines is fine).
- The launcher has a macOS path (`perl setsid`) that Rust would need to replace, so this is a migration, not a one-commit change.
- The deslop already cut the launcher from 3720 to 3173 lines, and the probe design (backgrounded, bounded read) is sound for what it does.
- I did not verify HYP: whether Rust `canonicalize` equals `pwd -P` on all paths, or that the Rust `no_hang` tests cover finding 5. I ran no tests.
```
